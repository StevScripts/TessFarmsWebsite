# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Indian immigrants and their families in the Lake Nona, St. Cloud, Kissimmee, and greater Orlando area. Core audience is first-generation Indian Americans who grew up with curry leaf trees, jasmine, mango, and parijat, and miss having them in their yards. Secondary audience is local tropical gardening enthusiasts who discover George through word of mouth.

Visitors arrive curious but cautious. They may have searched for "Indian plants near me" or been told about George by a neighbor. They want to know: does this person actually have the plants I'm looking for, and can I trust him? Many are older, not deeply technical, and may be browsing on a phone shared with family.

## Product Purpose

A public-facing website for Tes Farms LLC, a home nursery in Central Florida run by George, an Indian immigrant and educator. The site exists to:

1. Make George findable online for people searching for Indian plants in Central Florida
2. Show what's available (mango, jasmine, curry leaf, guava, parijat, and more) without exposing exact stock levels
3. Build trust and personal connection so visitors feel confident calling or WhatsApping George
4. Drive phone calls and WhatsApp messages, the primary conversion action

George operates by appointment from his property. There is no storefront, no e-commerce, no online ordering. The entire funnel ends with a phone call or WhatsApp message.

## Brand Personality

Warm, rooted, personal.

Like visiting a knowledgeable uncle's garden. George is an educator who genuinely loves teaching people how to grow these plants. The site should feel like a conversation with someone who knows exactly what you need and is happy to help. Community-first, never corporate. The kind of place where you'd bring your parents.

## Anti-references

- **Generic garden center sites**: stock photography of white suburban families in gardens, clip art icons, "shop now" buttons everywhere. No soul, no story, no cultural specificity.
- **Overly corporate/SaaS aesthetic**: minimal to the point of cold. Clean grids, faceless illustrations, startup language. Feels like a tech product, not a place with real soil.
- **Cluttered small business sites**: walls of text, busy layouts, inconsistent styling, flashing elements, dated design. The "we built this in 2008 and never updated it" look that undermines trust.
- **Instagram plant influencer aesthetic**: millennial pink, monstera everything, "plant mom" vibes. Wrong demographic, wrong tone.

## Design Principles

1. **Recognition before explanation.** The visitor should see themselves in this site within seconds. Plant names they know, a story they relate to, a feeling of "this person gets it." Don't explain Indian plants to an Indian audience.
2. **Trust through specificity.** Twelve named mango varieties builds more trust than "we have many mangoes." George's real name, real phone number, real YouTube channel. Specificity signals legitimacy.
3. **One action, everywhere.** Every page leads to the same place: call or WhatsApp George. Never hide the phone number. Never add friction between "I want this" and "I'm talking to George."
4. **Warmth over polish.** The site should feel like George's nursery feels: real, abundant, cared-for. Not sterile. Not overly designed. A place where personality comes through.
5. **Respect the visitor's time.** Many visitors are older, on mobile, possibly with limited English comfort. Large text, clear hierarchy, fast loading, no confusion about what to do next.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA compliance as baseline
- Large text sizes throughout: body text minimum 18px equivalent, generous line height
- High contrast ratios, especially for text on colored backgrounds
- Touch targets sized for older users on mobile (minimum 44x44px)
- Clear, simple language; avoid jargon and unnecessarily complex sentences
- Reduced motion respected via prefers-reduced-motion
- No bilingual content for now, but keep copy simple enough that it doesn't alienate non-native English readers
